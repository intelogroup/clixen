## Science

**1. Bell inequality violated using the motion of massive particles - a first**

In a preprint posted September 2 (arXiv:2609.02009), physicists from Australian National University and the University of Queensland report violation of a Bell inequality using momentum-entangled pairs of metastable helium atoms, manipulated by matter-wave interferometry. They measure a CHSH parameter of 2.52 (threshold 2(, with stated uncertainty about 0.17. Nonlocality had previously been shown only with photons or the internal states of massive particles, not with their motional degrees of freedom. The authors say the result opens a regime for probing the interplay between quantum mechanics and gravity. Source: arXiv - https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02009

**2. IBM shows off modular 'quantum fridge' about 180 times colder than deep space**

IBM revealed the next step of its long-running Project Goldeneye effort to scale cryogenic cooling for quantum computers. As described by Popular Mechanics, the system is a modular dilution-refrigeration design whose two connected modules stand roughly eight feet tall and cool to about 4 kelvin - roughly 180 times colder than deep space - in about five days. An L-coupler links chips across modules, letting fridges be chained together toward IBM's goal of a 1,000-qubit quantum computer by next year. Source: Popular Mechanics - https://www.popularmechanics.com/technology/a73571757/quantum-fridge/

**4. Space-grown tomatoes rewired thousands of genes, NASA study finds**



In a NASA-backed study, tomato plants grown in space switched thousands of genes, with light helping replace gravity as a growth signal. That is evidence of how crops adapt in microgravity, relevant to long-duration space agriculture. Source: Times of India - https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/science/tomato-plants-grown-in-space-switched-thousands-of-genes-nasa-study-reveals-how-light-helps-replace-gravity-as-a-growth-signal/articleshow/133704059.cms
