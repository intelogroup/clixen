## AI

**1. Z.ai's GLM-5.3-Flash runs entirely on Chinese-made chips; shares jump 8%**
Chinese AI company Z.ai released GLM-5.3-Flash, a low-cost model it says operates using only homegrown semiconductors — roughly 100,000 China-made chips handle all online requests, including when the model debuted Aug. 20 under the codename "Ox Alpha." The model ranks 10th on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index (ahead of DeepSeek V4 Pro Max), was the most-used model on OpenRouter last week, and sent Z.ai's Hong Kong-listed shares up more than 8%. CNBC notes it could not independently verify the claims.

Source: CNBC — https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/27/zai-shares-surge-new-ai-model-chinese-chips.html

**2. Meta returns to open-weight AI with Muse Glimmer, a 30B model for consumer hardware**
Meta unveiled Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter open-weight model optimized for local, always-on AI agents, as CEO Mark Zuckerberg argues capable AI should be widely available rather than controlled by a few institutions. Nvidia, which optimized the model for its hardware, says Glimmer can run on a PC with a single consumer GPU.

Source: Campus Technology — https://campustechnology.com/articles/2026/08/26/meta-releases-muse-glimmer-an-open-weight-ai-model-designed-to-run-on-consumer-hardware.aspx

**3. FSB chair warns frontier AI models threaten global financial stability**
In a letter to G20 finance ministers and central bank governors, Financial Stability Board Chair Andrew Bailey warned that frontier AI models — showing increasingly sophisticated autonomy and threat capabilities — pose growing risks, with cyber risk the most immediate concern. He called on authorities to support safe, responsible model release and deployment, and for financial institutions to harden response and recovery capabilities and resilience among critical third-party providers.

Source: FSB — https://www.fsb.org/2026/08/fsb-chair-warns-of-risks-arising-frontier-ai-models/

**4. Anthropic says AI risks are rising, has no plan to release stronger "Model 2"**
Anthropic's internal safety report describes an unreleased "Model 2" that shows a "noticeable improvement" on many internal tasks and is used heavily in-house for coding, agentic work and data generation — but the company says it currently has no plans to release it externally, citing rising risk. The performance jump is reportedly smaller than the leap from Opus 4.6 to Mythos earlier this year.

Source: Axios — https://www.axios.com/2026/08/14/anthropic-model-2-ai-risk

## Science

**1. NASA's Roman Space Telescope is in orbit: launch confirmed**
The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off Sunday (Aug. 30) aboard a SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center, beginning its mission to probe dark matter, dark energy and distant exoplanets. Roman will see 100 times more sky than Hubble in a single view, and its launch marks the start of a new era of wide-field cosmic surveys — an update to the pre-launch coverage in this morning's briefing.

Source: ScienceDaily — https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/08/260831035213.htm

**2. Stanford scientists regrow lost cartilage and reverse arthritis in mice**
A Stanford Medicine-led study found that blocking the aging-related protein 15-PGDH (or raising prostaglandin E2 levels) restored lost knee cartilage in older mice and prevented arthritis from developing after serious joint injuries. The same pathway also regenerated damaged muscle, nerve, bone, colon, liver and blood tissue, pointing toward a broadly applicable anti-aging tissue-repair strategy.

Source: ScienceDaily — https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/06/260612021.htm

**3. Undisclosed death in a Chinese gene-editing trial raises red flags**
A CNN investigation into China's booming gene-editing industry reports that a participant's death in a clinical trial was not disclosed, highlighting regulatory and ethical gaps that could become a stumbling block for Chinese biotech even as it rises as a global power. The report urges closer scrutiny of trial oversight and transparency.

Source: CNN — https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/30/china/china-death-gene-editing-trial

## Tech

**1. NVIDIA kicks off the "next generation of AI" with the Rubin platform**
At CES, NVIDIA launched the Rubin platform — six new chips designed to work as one AI supercomputer, aimed at building, deploying and securing the largest AI systems at lower cost. The lineup spans data-center, cloud and autonomous-machine segments and is positioned as the successor generation to Blackwell for mainstream AI adoption.

Source: NVIDIA News — https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/rubin-platform-ai-supercomputer

**2. UK unveils £1.1 billion AI Hardware Plan to back chip firms and compute**
Technology Secretary Liz Kendall announced the AI Hardware Plan at London Tech Week, committing £1.1 billion to back British chip and semiconductor companies, boost computing power and grow AI skills. It includes £120 million for a new AI Hardware Innovation Programme to help de-risk chip development, which can take years and cost millions before viability is proven.

Source: GOV.UK — https://www.gov.uk/government/news/a-decisive-shift-to-power-british-ai

**3. Valve's Steam Machine returns — this time as a home console**
Valve revived the Steam Machine in a surprise November 2025 announcement, this time as a home-console sibling to the Steam Deck rather than a manufacturer program, paired with a new Steam Controller and a wireless VR headset called the Steam Frame. Price and release date remain unannounced, but the console is expected to launch in 2026.

Source: Engadget — https://www.engadget.com/gaming/pc/valves-steam-machine-launches-in-2026
