## Science

**1. "Not-quite-primordial" black holes may explain a baffling JWST discovery**
 New Scientist reports that astronomers have identified a population of intermediate-mass black holes — denser than stellar remnants yet far smaller than galactic centers — that could account for an otherwise puzzling JWST observation in the early universe. The objects are not classic primordial black holes, but their inferred masses fit a long-sought bridge between stellar and supermassive black holes. Source: New Scientist — https://www.newscientist.com/article/2587559-not-quite-primordial-black-holes-may-explain-baffling-jwst-discovery/

**2. AI-enhanced virtual screening accelerates drug discovery from large libraries**
 A study published in Nature Biotechnology describes an adaptive virtual-screening pipeline that combines machine learning with biophysical modeling to prioritize the most promising ligand candidates from libraries of millions of compounds — cutting hit-identification time and cost relative to traditional high-throughput screens. The authors demonstrate the method on several druggable targets, reporting a notable enrichment of experimentally active compounds. Source: Nature Biotechnology — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03217-x

**3. Engineered genomic attachment sites enable efficient recombinase integration in plants and human cells**
 Researchers report in Nature Biotechnology the design of synthetic genomic "landing pads" — attachment sites tuned for site-specific recombinases — that enable high-efficiency, reproducible genetic integration in both plant and human cell contexts. The system sidesteps positional effects common to random integration and could speed development of gene therapies, crop traits, and functional genomics studies. Source: Nature Biotechnology — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41587-026-03294-y
