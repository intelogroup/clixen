## Science

**1. MeerKAT directly detects a faint neutral-hydrogen signal from the distant universe**
Astronomers from the University of Manchester and the University of the Western Cape used South Africa's MeerKAT radio telescope to directly detect radio emission from neutral hydrogen gas billions of light-years away — dating to when the universe was several billion years younger — opening a powerful new way to map the large-scale structure of the cosmos without relying on brighter tracers. Source: Phys.org — https://phys.org/news/2026-09-meerkat-faint-hydrogen-distant-universe.html

**2. Gravity tested across galaxy clusters hundreds of millions of light-years apart — matches Newton and Einstein**
A new study finds gravity behaves almost exactly as Newtonian–Einsteinian gravity predicts when measured across galaxy clusters separated by hundreds of millions of light-years, even though stars in the outer regions of galaxies move far faster than expected — a result researchers say strengthens the case for dark matter over modified-gravity alternatives. Source: The Times of India — https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/science/researchers-tested-gravity-across-galaxy-clusters-separated-by-hundreds-of-millions-of-light-years-and-found-it-behaves-almost-exactly-as-newton-and-einstein-predicted-strengthening-the-case-for-dark-matter/articleshow/133705265.cms

A global network of the world's most powerful telescopes has revealed a small, dark, invisible object — the lowest-mass dark object ever found — detectable only through the way its gravity distorts space; authors say such objects could shed light on the nature of dark matter.(Distinct from yesterday's LUX-ZEPLIN WIMP candidate — this one is lensing-based..
