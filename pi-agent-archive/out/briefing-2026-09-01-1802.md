## Science

**1. Moscow State University sets its sights on Apophis's 2029 close approach**
The roughly 350-meter asteroid, discovered in 2004, will come within about 32,000 km of Earth on April 13, 2029—closer than the altitude of many geostationary satellites. The Nuclear Physics Research Institute of Moscow State University says it is already exploring launching small spacecraft or CubeSats to study the encounter, which it frames as a breakthrough in space research. Source: MSU News (RU)— https://www1.ru/en/news/2026/09/01/novyi-proryv-v-kosmose-sblizenie-asteroida-apofis-s-zemlei-izucit-mgu.html



**2. An accordion's inner workings inspire a heart-flow device dubbed "Hemadyne""**
Following a graduate student's observation about the instrument, researchers developed a portable device that reproduces realistic waveforms of human blood flow for organ-chip and vascular research, with potential applications ranging from clinical trials to space medicine—NASA is reportedly interested in durable versions to replace animals in microgravity studies..



Source: Medscape — https://www.medscape.com/viewarticle/polka-music-scientific-breakthrough-accordions-unlikely-2026a1000tbb

**2. Hong Kong team builds a 2D tunnelling transistor aimed at faster AI chips**
A PolyU-led team reports an ultra-thin Bi/InSe tunnelling transistor that switches below the conventional 60-millivolt/decade limit across six orders of magnitude, requiring a gate-voltage range of just 160 millivolts. The device pairs high output current with a strong on/off ratio, and was fabricated on centimetre-scale silicon substrates via a process the researchers say could scale toward production of energy-efficient AI and computing chips. Source: The Quantum Insider — https://thequantuminsider.com/2026/09/01/quantum-tunnelling-field-effect-transistor-designed-to-overcome-integrated-circuit-chip-development-barriers
