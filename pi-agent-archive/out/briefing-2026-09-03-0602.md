## AI

**1. New York City imposes year-long generative-AI ban for younger students**
 NYC public schools will bar student-facing generative AI tools for students through eighth grade this school year — a one-year moratorium affecting roughly 600,000 students and halting about 40 educational AI tools — while high schoolers get AI literacy classes and supervised pilot programs. Mayor Zohran Mamdani called the move the most expansive such prohibition in the US. Source: DW — https://www.dw.com/en/us-new-york-city-mayor-mamdani-announces-ban-on-ai-for-younger-students/a-78815448

**2. Tencent releases Hy4 Preview, a 770B-parameter open-weight MoE model**
 Tencent's Hunyuan team released Hy4 Preview under Apache 2.0 on Hugging Face, ModelScope and GitCode: 770B total parameters with only 49B active per token, a 1M-token context window, and new Gated DeepSeek Sparse Attention with IndexCache aimed at long-horizon coding and agentic work. In blind internal evaluations it narrowly outperformed GLM-5.3 and Kimi K3 on engineering tasks. Source: MindStudio — https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/tencent-hy4-preview-model

**3. AI plotted an interstellar trajectory to Alpha Centauri; PSI launches with $58M**
 The nonprofit Fermi Explorer Mission says it plans to launch a spacecraft toward Alpha Centauri (4.4 light-years away) by the end of 2029, following a novel trajectory discovered by an AI system from Physical Superintelligence (PSI) — an AI-physics lab launching today with $58M in funding led by Bill Gates' Breakthrough Energy. The journey could take up to 80,000 years. Source: MIT Technology Review — https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/09/01/1143247/ai-interstellar-journey-alpha-centauri/

**4. PACMAN AI framework makes split-second fusion-control decisions**
 Researchers at Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory and Princeton University unveiled PACMAN, an AI software framework that makes key decisions in milliseconds to keep fusion plasmas stable — faster than any human operator — while keeping humans in charge of goals. It targets instabilities that can develop in a few thousandths of a second. Source: phys.org — https://phys.org/news/2026-09-pacman-ai-framework-fusion-safely.html

## Science

**1. Einstein's gravity observed acting on a falling quantum object — for the first time**
 An international team led by Ben-Gurion University, Ulm and Oxford — including Nobel laureate Sir Roger Penrose — reports the first observation of a long-predicted gravitational effect on a falling quantum object, using an atom chip. Published Sept. 2 in Science Advances, the result shows a core principle of Einstein's gravity remains consistent with quantum behavior. Source: phys.org — https://phys.org/news/2026-09-scientists-einstein-gravity-quantum-world.html

**2. New nanostructure makes non-linear light conversion 72,000× more efficient**
 Researchers from TU Graz, Harvard and UT Austin combined a semiconductor layer with a metasurface to convert light into new frequencies about 72,000 times more efficiently than previous materials — a result published in Nature Nanotechnology that could advance telecommunications and quantum technology. Source: phys.org — https://phys.org/news/2026-09-nanostructure-linear-conversion-efficient.html

**3. AI-designed proteins enable a new generation of RNA transporters**
 Helmholtz research teams report in Nature a bottom-up-assembled "STV" RNA carrier built from AI-designed proteins, offering a potential alternative to virus-derived vehicles and lipid nanoparticles for delivering RNA therapeutics into cells — more efficiently and, in future, more selectively. Source: phys.org — https://phys.org/news/2026-09-ai-proteins-enable-generation-rna.html

**4. First fully solvent-free perovskite-silicon tandem solar cell hits 27.3%**
 A University of Freiburg / Fraunhofer ISE team, with partners in Halle, Seville and KAUST, fabricated the first perovskite-silicon tandem cell made entirely without solvents, using thermal evaporation. It reached a champion efficiency of 27.3% (27.0% stabilized) and retained 97.06% of initial efficiency after 6,800 hours of dark storage — a route that avoids toxic solvents and eases industrial upscaling. Source: Perovskite-Info — https://www.perovskite-info.com/first-fully-solvent-free-perovskite-silicon-tandem-solar-cell-reaches-273

It acts as a Matter 1.5 controller supporting 40+ device categories, 16 wireless and 4 wired cameras, and 34 sensors, with planned "agents" for energy and robot vacuums. **2. IBM releases Nighthawk r2, its fastest QPU yet**
 IBM Quantum announced Nighthawk r2 (deployed as ibm_phoenix), a 120-qubit processor on a square-lattice architecture with a high-speed dissipative qubit-reset framework that eliminates long idle periods — achieving over 100,000 circuits per second, a 25× throughput gain over the Heron fleet. Source: Quantum Computing Report — https://quantumcomputingreport.com/ibm-releases-nighthawk-r2-qpu-featuring-active-dissipative-qubit-reset-and-25x-circuit-throughput

**3. MIT designs a dual-purpose qubit for faster, more stable circuits**
 MIT researchers proposed a qubit architecture with two separate but connected components — one storing data, one reaching out to interact with the rest of the circuit — letting qubits interact much more quickly while staying stable. The design could be a step toward scalable, practical quantum computers that run long, complex algorithms accurately. Source: MIT News — https://news.mit.edu/2026/new-qubit-architecture-enables-faster-more-accurate-operations-0903
